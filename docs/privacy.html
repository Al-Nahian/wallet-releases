Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

This policy explains what data the Wallet Android app ("Wallet", "the app") collects, how it's used, and what choices you have. Wallet is built to be local-first: using the app does not require an account, and most people will never have any data leave their device.

Data stored locally on your device

All the financial data you enter — accounts, transactions, categories, budgets, recurring payments, savings goals, labels, and templates — is stored in a local database on your device. This data is never transmitted anywhere unless you explicitly choose to sign in (see below).

SMS access (optional)

If you grant the SMS permission, Wallet reads incoming SMS messages on-device to automatically detect and pre-fill transactions from bank and mobile-wallet alerts (e.g. bKash, Nagad, Rocket, bank debit/credit notifications). This parsing happens entirely on your device:

  • Raw SMS content is never stored permanently and never leaves your device.
  • Only the extracted transaction summary (amount, merchant/payee, date) is kept, and only after you review and confirm it (or, if you've explicitly marked a sender as trusted, it's added automatically and you're notified with an option to undo).
  • This permission is off by default and optional — you can use Wallet fully by entering transactions manually if you prefer not to grant it. It's requested only when you turn on "Enable SMS detection" in Automation Settings, never on first launch.
  • So that one payment isn't recorded twice when it arrives as both an SMS and a notification, Wallet keeps a one-way fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash) of each captured message, together with the extracted amount and accounts, for 7 days. The message text can't be recovered from the fingerprint, it never leaves your device, and it's deleted automatically after 7 days.

Notification access (optional automation feature)

Wallet offers a second, separate way to catch transactions SMS alone might miss, like a mobile-wallet app's own cashback/reward notifications: reading notifications posted by other apps on your device. This is off by default and requires two explicit steps — turning on "Enable notification detection" in Automation Settings, then granting Wallet "Notification access" in your device's system Settings (a special, high-trust Android permission separate from the ordinary notifications permission below).

  • Because of how this Android permission works, once granted, Wallet's notification listener technically receives the content of every notification posted on your device, not just banking ones — this is a platform requirement of the permission type, not something Wallet can narrow in advance.
  • In practice, each notification's text is checked in memory, on-device, against patterns for recognized bank/mobile-wallet messages; anything that doesn't match is discarded immediately and never stored, logged, or transmitted anywhere.
  • Only a matched transaction's extracted summary (amount, merchant/payee, date) is kept, the same way as SMS capture above, and the same review/trusted-sender flow applies.
  • You can revoke this access at any time from your device's Settings → Apps → Special app access → Notification access.

App notifications you receive

Separately, the ordinary notifications permission is used only for the app's own in-app alerts — budget warnings, upcoming recurring payments, goal milestones, captured-transaction confirmations — and is never used to deliver advertising or third-party content.

Optional account sign-in and cloud sync

Signing in is entirely optional and is never required to use the app. If you choose to sign in with Google, Microsoft, or an email/password, Wallet uses Supabase as its authentication and backend provider. Signing in:

  • Stores a basic profile (display name, email address, and an avatar — either one provided by Google/Microsoft, one of Wallet's built-in preset icons, or a custom photo you upload) tied to your account, used only to identify your account within Wallet. A custom photo you upload is stored only on your device, never uploaded to our backend.
  • Backs up your financial data — accounts, transactions, categories, budgets, recurring payments, goals, debts, labels and templates — plus a few app settings (theme, and whether SMS/notification detection is switched on), so you can restore them on another device. The backup is sent over an encrypted connection and stored encrypted at rest by our backend provider. For protection that also covers the people running the service, see "End-to-end encryption" below.
  • Keeps a short activity log tied to your account (e.g. "Transaction amount changed from BDT 500.00 to BDT 650.00") so changes made from different devices can be reconciled correctly — never your raw SMS or notification text, and visible only to you.
  • Your data is protected by database-level access rules (Row Level Security) so that only your own signed-in account can read or write it — never other users. These rules don't apply to the service's administrators, so unless you turn on end-to-end encryption, the developer and our backend provider can technically access your backed-up data. We don't look at it, but we'd rather you not have to take our word for it — that's what end-to-end encryption is for.
  • Records which device currently holds your sign-in. Your account can be signed in on one device at a time: signing in on a new device signs the previous one out.

End-to-end encryption (optional)

You can turn on end-to-end encryption in your Profile. When it's on, your financial data and synced settings are encrypted on your device before they're uploaded, with a key only you hold. Nobody else can read them — not the developer, not our backend provider, and not anyone with direct access to the database.

  • When you turn it on, Wallet creates a 12-word recovery phrase and shows it to you once. The encryption key is derived from that phrase on your device. The phrase is never sent to us, and we can't recover or reset it. If you lose it, your encrypted backup can't be restored on a new device.
  • To unlock your backup on a new device, you enter your recovery phrase. On a device you've already unlocked, the key is kept in the device's secure hardware-backed key store.
  • What stays readable to the service: technical details needed to sync — record IDs, when records were created or changed, which records belong together (for example, which account a transaction belongs to), and your profile details (name, email, avatar). Amounts, descriptions, names, notes and dates are encrypted.
  • Data you'd already backed up is converted when you turn encryption on, and the readable copies are removed from the live database. Our backend provider keeps routine backups for a limited time, so earlier copies may remain there until those backups expire.

Signing out and deleting your data

Signing out removes your data from the device, so the next person to use it can't see your finances. If any changes haven't been backed up yet, Wallet offers to back them up first, and asks you to confirm before deleting anything that hasn't been backed up.

If your sign-in ends without you signing out — for example, because you signed in on another device — your data stays on the device, hidden behind the sign-in screen, until you sign back in with the same account or choose to remove it.

You can request deletion of your cloud account and all associated data at any time by contacting us (see below).

What we don't do

  • No advertising, and no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs are included in the app.
  • We never sell or share your financial data with third parties.
  • We don't use SMS or notification access for anything other than on-device transaction parsing — both are off by default and opt-in.

Children's privacy

Wallet is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.
